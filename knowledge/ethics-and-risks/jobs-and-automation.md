**In short:** robots and AI can take over tasks people do, raise the
productivity of others, and create new work. Studies agree that many jobs are
*exposed*; they disagree about how many will be *lost*, and about how large the
economic gains will be.

## Explanation

### Measured effects of robots

Acemoglu and Restrepo studied the rise of industrial robots in US local labour
markets between 1990 and 2007. They estimate that one more robot per thousand
workers reduced the employment-to-population ratio by about 0.18–0.34
percentage points and wages by 0.25–0.5%.[^acemoglu-restrepo]

### Exposure estimates

- **2013, computerisation.** Frey and Osborne estimated the probability of
  computerisation for 702 occupations and concluded that about 47% of total US
  employment was at risk.[^frey-osborne]
- **2023, language models.** Eloundou et al. estimated that around 80% of the
  US workforce could have at least 10% of their tasks affected by large
  language models, and about 19% at least half of their tasks. They made no
  prediction of timing.[^eloundou]
- **2025, global.** The ILO finds that one in four workers worldwide is in an
  occupation with some exposure to generative AI, but only 3.3% are in the
  highest-exposure category — 4.7% of women's employment against 2.4% of
  men's. Exposure is 34% of employment in high-income countries versus 11% in
  low-income ones.[^ilo-genai-2025]

"Exposed" does not mean "replaced": the ILO concludes that "transformation of
jobs is the most likely impact of GenAI", because most occupations still need
human input.[^ilo-genai-2025]

### Productivity

Among 5,179 customer-support agents, a generative AI assistant raised
productivity by 14% on average and by 34% for novice and low-skilled
workers.[^brynjolfsson]

### How big is the economic effect? A disagreement

Daron Acemoglu argues the macroeconomic effects will be "nontrivial but
modest" — no more than about 0.66% higher total factor productivity over ten
years, and possibly under 0.53% — and that AI may increase inequality even when
it helps low-skill workers.[^acemoglu-macro] Others expect much larger effects;
see [possible impact of AGI](../agi/impact.md).

## Key points

- Robots measurably lowered local US employment and wages,
  1990–2007.[^acemoglu-restrepo]
- Exposure estimates are large, but exposure is not job
  loss.[^frey-osborne][^eloundou][^ilo-genai-2025]
- AI assistants help the least-experienced workers most.[^brynjolfsson]
- Economists disagree on the size of the overall gain.[^acemoglu-macro]
