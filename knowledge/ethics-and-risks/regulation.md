**In short:** AI regulation is developing fast and differently around the
world. The EU has a binding, risk-based law; the US relies mainly on voluntary
frameworks and a pro-innovation strategy; China regulates specific services
such as generative AI; and international bodies agree on shared principles.

## Explanation

### European Union: the AI Act

The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is a comprehensive, binding AI
law.[^eu-ai-act] It entered into force on 1 August 2024 and sorts AI by risk:[^eu-ai-act-overview]

| Risk level | What the Act does | Applies from |
| ---------- | ----------------- | ------------ |
| Unacceptable | Bans practices such as social scoring and harmful manipulation | February 2025 |
| General-purpose AI models | Obligations for providers; extra duties for models with systemic risk | 2 August 2025 |
| Transparency | Disclosure duties, e.g. telling people they are talking to a chatbot | August 2026 |
| High risk (sensitive uses, Annex III) | Strict obligations for uses such as biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment and border control | 2 December 2027 |
| High risk (inside regulated products, Annex I) | Same obligations for AI built into products such as machinery | 2 August 2028 |
| Minimal | No new rules — the vast majority of AI systems | — |

The high-risk dates were pushed back by the "AI Omnibus" simplification
regulation: the Commission proposed it on 19 November 2025, a political
agreement was reached on 7 May 2026, and it entered into force on 27 July
2026.[^eu-ai-act-overview] From 2 August 2026 the EU AI Office and national
authorities supervise and enforce the Act, and the AI Office holds enforcement
powers over general-purpose AI models.[^eu-ai-act-overview] A
voluntary General-Purpose AI Code of Practice, published on 10 July 2025, helps
providers show compliance on transparency, copyright, and safety and
security.[^gpai-code]

### United States

The July 2025 AI Action Plan sets out more than 90 federal policy actions under
three pillars: accelerating innovation, building American AI infrastructure,
and leading in international diplomacy and security.[^us-ai-action-plan] NIST's
AI Risk Management Framework, released in January 2023, is intended for
voluntary use.[^nist-ai-rmf]

### China

China's Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services took
effect on 15 August 2023, regulating generative AI services offered to the
public.[^china-genai-measures]

### International principles

- The **OECD AI Principles**, adopted in 2019 and updated in May 2024, were the
  first intergovernmental standard on AI and have 47
  adherents.[^oecd-principles]
- In November 2021 UNESCO's 193 Member States adopted the
  **Recommendation on the Ethics of AI**, the first global standard on AI
  ethics.[^unesco-ethics]
- The G7's **Hiroshima AI Process**, launched in May 2023, produced guiding
  principles and a code of conduct for advanced AI systems.[^hiroshima]

## Key points

- The EU AI Act phases in risk-based rules from 2025 to
  2028.[^eu-ai-act-overview]
- The US emphasises innovation and voluntary
  frameworks.[^us-ai-action-plan][^nist-ai-rmf]
- China regulates generative AI services directly.[^china-genai-measures]
- OECD, UNESCO and G7 principles provide shared
  baselines.[^oecd-principles][^unesco-ethics][^hiroshima]
