**In short:** autonomous weapons systems — sometimes called "killer robots" —
are weapons that select and apply force to targets based on sensor processing,
without a human deciding each attack. Most countries support international
action on them, but there is not yet a treaty.

## Explanation

### What they are

Human Rights Watch describes autonomous weapons systems as systems that "select
and apply force to targets based on sensor processing rather than human
inputs".[^hrw-2024] The core concern is removing human control from the use of
force.[^hrw-2024]

### The UN process

- In December 2023 the UN General Assembly adopted its first resolution on the
  dangers of lethal autonomous weapons, which asked the Secretary-General for a
  report. That report, released in August 2024, examined the issue "from
  humanitarian, legal, security, technological and ethical
  perspectives".[^hrw-2024]
- On 2 December 2024 the Assembly adopted resolution 79/62 by 166 votes to 3
  (Belarus, North Korea and Russia), with 15 abstentions.[^hrw-2024][^un-res-79-62]
- The resolution noted the Secretary-General's repeated calls to urgently
  conclude negotiations on a legally binding instrument, but did not itself
  open negotiations. It set up open informal consultations in New York during
  2025.[^hrw-2024]
- Talks under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons in Geneva have
  run since 2014 without a substantive outcome; that forum works by consensus,
  so a single country can block proposals.[^hrw-2024]

### What treaty supporters want

Most treaty proponents call for prohibiting autonomous weapons that operate
without meaningful human control or that target people, and for regulations so
that all other autonomous weapons cannot be used without meaningful human
control.[^hrw-2024]

### The wider risk debate

AI-safety researchers list military use among catastrophic risks: the
"malicious use" and "AI race" categories describe how competition could push
actors to deploy unsafe AI or cede control to it.[^hendrycks]

## Key points

- Autonomous weapons select and apply force without a human deciding each
  attack.[^hrw-2024]
- The UN voted 166–3 in December 2024 for further discussion, not
  negotiation.[^hrw-2024][^un-res-79-62]
- Treaty supporters seek "meaningful human control".[^hrw-2024]
