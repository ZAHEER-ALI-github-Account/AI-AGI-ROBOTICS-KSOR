**In short:** farming is being automated with machinery, robots and digital
tools — from milking robots to systems that use machine learning. Automation
can raise productivity and resilience, but the UN's food agency warns it must
be inclusive, because small-scale producers face real barriers to adopting it.

## Explanation

### The FAO's view

The FAO's *State of Food and Agriculture 2022* looks at how automation in
agrifood systems can help achieve the Sustainable Development Goals and how
policy can maximise benefits and minimise risks.[^fao-sofa-2022] It draws on 27
case studies from around the world — from hire-a-tractor services in Ghana to
shrimp-farming boxes in Mexico that use machine learning and
robotics.[^fao-sofa-2022]

The report identifies barriers that prevent adoption, particularly by
small-scale producers, and recommends policies so that agricultural automation
is inclusive and contributes to sustainable and resilient agrifood
systems.[^fao-sofa-2022]

### Agricultural robots in numbers

The International Federation of Robotics counted close to 19,500 agricultural
robots sold for professional use in 2024, down 6%, with the decrease driven
mainly by cultivation and milking robots.[^ifr-service-2025]
