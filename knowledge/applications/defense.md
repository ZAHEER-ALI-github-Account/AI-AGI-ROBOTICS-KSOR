**In short:** governments now treat AI and robotics as matters of national
power, and state institutes test AI models for national-security risks. The
sharpest debate is over weapons that select and engage targets without direct
human control.

## Explanation

### AI as power

The US government's July 2025 AI Action Plan calls AI "a revolutionary
technology with the potential to transform the global economy and alter the
balance of power in the world", and says that "to remain the leading economic
and military power, the United States must win the AI race".[^us-ai-action-plan]

### Security evaluations of AI models

The UK's AI Security Institute describes itself as the first state-backed
organisation dedicated to ensuring advanced AI is safe, secure and
beneficial.[^aisi] In the US, NIST's center (now listed as CAISSI) says it will
"lead unclassified evaluations" of advanced AI capabilities that may pose risks
to national security, focusing on cybersecurity, biosecurity and chemical
weapons.[^caissi]

### Autonomy in weapons

Human Rights Watch describes autonomous weapons systems as systems that "select
and apply force to targets based on sensor processing" rather than human
input.[^hrw-2024] On 2 December 2024 the UN General Assembly adopted resolution
79/62 on lethal autonomous weapons systems by 166 votes to 3, with 15
abstentions. It did not mandate treaty negotiations; it set up open informal
consultations in New York during 2025.[^hrw-2024] See
[autonomous weapons](../ethics-and-risks/autonomous-weapons.md).

### Frontier risks

The 2023 Bletchley Declaration, agreed by countries at the first AI Safety
Summit, said particular safety risks arise at the "frontier" of AI — highly
capable general-purpose models.[^bletchley]

### Open question

Open question: how specific armed forces currently use AI and robots (for
example in logistics, intelligence or uncrewed vehicles) is not covered by
these sources and should be added with official citations.

## Key points

- Governments frame AI leadership in economic and military
  terms.[^us-ai-action-plan]
- State institutes evaluate advanced AI for national-security
  risks.[^aisi][^caissi]
- The UN voted 166–3 in 2024 to keep discussing autonomous weapons, without a
  treaty mandate.[^hrw-2024]
