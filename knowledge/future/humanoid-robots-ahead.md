**In short:** humanoids are moving from demonstrations to production lines.
Manufacturers are building them for scale, carmakers are validating them in
factories, and analysts forecast a market worth tens of billions of dollars.
Whether that happens depends on robot intelligence, reliability and cost.

## Explanation

### Built for volume

- Boston Dynamics unveiled the product version of Atlas in January 2026.[^atlas-2026]
- Figure says Figure 03 was "engineered from the ground-up for high-volume
  manufacturing", with a new supply chain and production process at its BotQ
  facility.[^figure-03]

### Into the factory, step by step

Hyundai Motor Group plans phased deployment of Atlas starting with
parts-sequencing tasks at its Georgia plant, with mass production and wider
deployment from 2028 after process-by-process validation at a dedicated
training centre.[^hyundai-ces-2026]

### Market forecasts

Goldman Sachs Research projects a $38 billion humanoid market by 2035 — more
than six times its earlier $6 billion estimate — and a base case of more than
250,000 humanoid shipments in 2030, almost all industrial, with consumer sales
exceeding a million units a year in just over a decade.[^goldman-humanoid]
These are one firm's projections, not measurements.

### What still has to be solved

- **Intelligence.** Humanoids need foundation models that can "reason about
  novel situations, robustly handle real-world variability, and rapidly learn
  new tasks".[^gr00t]
- **Generalisation.** Task-specific robots struggle when the world differs from
  their training.[^fm-survey]

### The long view

Japan's Moonshot programme envisions "general-purpose autonomous humanoid AI
robots" supporting every aspect of human society by 2050.[^moonshot-goal3]

## Key points

- Humanoids are now designed for mass production.[^atlas-2026][^figure-03]
- Factory deployment is phased and validated, with wider rollout from 2028 at
  Hyundai.[^hyundai-ces-2026]
- Forecasts are large but uncertain.[^goldman-humanoid]
- Robot intelligence and generalisation are the bottlenecks.[^gr00t][^fm-survey]
