**In short:** since 2023 governments have moved from general AI principles to
specific action on the most capable "frontier" models: international summits,
state institutes that test models, a scientific report modelled on climate
assessments, national strategies that frame AI as a race, and new UN bodies.

## Explanation

### The summit series

- **Bletchley, November 2023.** Countries attending the first AI Safety Summit
  agreed that particular safety risks arise at the "frontier" of AI — highly
  capable general-purpose models.[^bletchley]
- **Seoul, May 2024.** Leading AI companies agreed the Frontier AI Safety
  Commitments.[^seoul-commitments]
- **Paris, February 2025.** The AI Action Summit produced a statement on
  inclusive and sustainable AI for people and the planet.[^paris-statement]

### An international scientific report

The Bletchley summit mandated an International AI Safety Report, chaired by
Yoshua Bengio. The first (January 2025) drew on 100 experts, with
representatives nominated by 30 nations, the UN, the OECD and the
EU.[^iasr-2025] The 2026 edition, published in February 2026, synthesises the
evidence on the capabilities, emerging risks and safety of general-purpose AI,
with over 100 experts and representatives from 29 nations, the UN, the OECD
and the EU.[^iasr-2026]

### Institutes that test AI models

The UK's AI Security Institute calls itself the first state-backed
organisation dedicated to making advanced AI safe, secure and
beneficial.[^aisi] In the US, NIST's center — now presented as the Center for
Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence — describes itself as
industry's primary point of contact in government for testing commercial
"super intelligence" systems, and publishes assessments of specific models,
including joint work with the UK institute.[^caissi]

### National strategies

- **United States.** The July 2025 AI Action Plan lists over 90 federal policy
  actions under three pillars — innovation, infrastructure, and international
  diplomacy and security.[^us-ai-action-plan] The White House's SI.gov site
  states "America is in a race for Super Intelligence. We intend to win it",
  and highlights the Genesis Mission, launched 24 November 2025, an
  "SI-for-Science" effort.[^si-gov]
- **China.** China released a Global AI Governance Action Plan on 26 July 2025
  at the World AI Conference in Shanghai.[^china-action-plan]
- **European Union.** Obligations for general-purpose AI models under the AI
  Act became applicable on 2 August 2025, and the EU AI Office holds
  enforcement powers over them.[^eu-ai-act-overview]

### The United Nations

On 26 August 2025 the UN General Assembly set the terms for two new bodies: an
**Independent International Scientific Panel on AI** of 40 members, and a
**Global Dialogue on AI Governance**, to meet first in Geneva in 2026 and New
York in 2027.[^un-res-79-325][^un-gdc-ai]

## Key points

- Summits moved from risk recognition (2023) to company commitments (2024) to
  inclusion (2025).[^bletchley][^seoul-commitments][^paris-statement]
- An international scientific report tracks frontier AI risks.[^iasr-2025][^iasr-2026]
- US policy frames AI and "super intelligence" as a race to
  win.[^us-ai-action-plan][^si-gov]
- The UN created a scientific panel and a global dialogue in
  2025.[^un-res-79-325]
