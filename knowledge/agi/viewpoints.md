**In short:** people who study AI disagree deeply about AGI. **Optimists**
expect it soon and expect large benefits. **Skeptics** doubt current methods
lead to general intelligence or expect AI to behave like an ordinary
technology. **Safety researchers** take extreme risks seriously and call for
stronger safeguards. Many experts hold parts of several views at once.

## Explanation

### Optimists

- Anthropic's Dario Amodei writes that he is not a "doomer" and sketches what
  "a country of geniuses in a datacenter" could do for biology, health and the
  economy if things go right.[^amodei-mlg]
- OpenAI writes that AGI "has the potential to give everyone incredible new
  capabilities" and wants it to "empower humanity to maximally flourish in the
  universe", while acknowledging "massive risks".[^openai-planning-agi]
- Researchers studying an early GPT-4 argued it showed "sparks" of general
  intelligence.[^sparks]

### Skeptics

- Gary Marcus argues deep learning alone will not reach AGI and must be
  supplemented by other techniques.[^marcus]
- François Chollet argues that benchmark skill can be "bought" with data and
  does not measure general intelligence.[^chollet]
- Bender, Gebru and colleagues describe language models as "stochastic
  parrots" that stitch together text "without any reference to
  meaning".[^stochastic-parrots]
- Narayanan and Kapoor propose seeing AI as "normal technology" rather than a
  potential superintelligence.[^normal-technology]

### Safety researchers

- A one-sentence statement from the Center for AI Safety, whose signatories
  include leading AI professors and company leaders, reads: "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority
  alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear
  war."[^cais-statement]
- A March 2023 open letter called on labs to "immediately pause for at least 6
  months the training of AI systems more powerful than
  GPT-4".[^fli-pause]
- A consensus paper led by Yoshua Bengio warns of risks including "an
  irreversible loss of human control over autonomous AI systems" and says AI
  safety research is lagging.[^bengio-science]

### What the wider field thinks

The largest researcher survey found that 68.3% thought good outcomes from
superhuman AI more likely than bad — yet between 38% and 51% gave at least a 10%
chance to outcomes as bad as human extinction, and there was broad agreement
that research to minimise risks should be prioritised more.[^grace-survey]

## Key points

- Optimists expect large benefits soon.[^amodei-mlg][^openai-planning-agi]
- Skeptics question whether current methods lead to AGI at
  all.[^marcus][^chollet][^normal-technology]
- Safety researchers call extinction-level risk a global
  priority.[^cais-statement][^bengio-science]
- Most researchers are hopeful but want more safety
  research.[^grace-survey]
